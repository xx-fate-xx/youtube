# Retention checklist (script stage, before CHECKPOINT 1)

Distilled from AgriciDaniel/claude-youtube (MIT; references/retention-scripting-guide.md, shorts-playbook.md,
sub-skills/script.md, hook.md, analyze.md; read 2026-11-23, not installed). Its percentages are unsourced
marketing statistics — we take the **techniques**, not the numbers. Our own rules still win (true facts, one
number per scene for What If, voice on top, no frozen pictures).

## Long video (8–15 min)

1. **0:00–0:05 = the thumbnail, moving.** Home-feed autoplay means the first seconds sell the click again:
   show the thumbnail's promise at once (speed → streaks, frozen world). No logo, no title card, no greeting,
   no "in this video".
2. **Hook 0:00–0:30 in three beats:** Grab (0–5 s: the most surprising true moment) → Promise (5–15 s: what
   the viewer will see, concretely) → Stakes (15–30 s: why it matters / what goes wrong).
3. **Open loop across the whole video ("suspension bridge").** Tease the best payoff in the hook ("the last
   rescue is the only one physics lets you win") and pay it off at the end. Every chapter ends with a forward
   hook into the next ("but that only works if they're falling. Fire doesn't wait.").
4. **Order chapters by strength:** strongest first and last, weaker ones in the middle, each opened with a
   change of format. Put a **re-hook at ~60 %** — it must feel like a second hook (new stakes, a twist).
5. **Pattern interrupt every ~30 s** (format, not just a new picture): freeze/chalk → slow motion → POV →
   reaction joke → diagram → music change. The picture itself changes every 4–8 s. No stretch > 20 s of dense
   explanation on one picture.
6. **The first minute after the hook is the danger zone** — deliver the first payoff (an action scene, a
   result) before any explanation. Explanations come after the viewer has seen why they matter.
7. **CTA:** one short soft line (≤ 8 s) right after the first payoff at ~20–25 %, tied to the content; the end is
   a next-video tease + end screen, never "thanks for watching".
8. **Chapters** in the description (0:00 first) — kept by `studio pipe release`.
9. **Retention risk map** in the script notes: list every spot with a long explanation, a run of numbers, a
   slow transition or a chapter > 90 s without a format change, each with its fix.

## Shorts (≤ 60 s)

1. **Hook in the first 1–3 s, picture first:** the first frame must already show the action; the first
   sentence is the question or the shock, no setup.
2. **Visual change at least every ~3 s.**
3. **Loop ending:** the last 1–2 s lead back into the first frame/line so a replay feels seamless.
4. One idea, payoff before the bridge line to the long video. Expect few clicks to the long video (people
   who watch Shorts mostly stay in the Shorts feed) — Shorts grow the channel; the long video is found via
   search/suggested.
5. Title: 4–6 words, keyword first, ≤ 40 visible characters.

## Reading the analytics (when the user sends screenshots)

- Shorts: **Viewed vs Swiped away** — ≥ 75 % good, < 60 % → rework the opening; average % viewed > 100 % = loops.
- Long: retention at 30 s ≥ 70 % good; < 50 % at 10–15 s = the hook fails. Curve shapes: cliff at the start →
  hook; valley at 40–60 % → pacing / move a strong part there; bumps → rewatched moments, make more of them;
  steady slope → normal.
- Fewer than ~100 views = no conclusions yet.
