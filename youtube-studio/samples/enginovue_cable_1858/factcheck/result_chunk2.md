## Notes

1. **Units.** The cable lengths in the sources (2,500 miles made; 380 or 335 miles laid; "1 ton per mile") are **nautical** miles. The opening of the narration ("close to 2,000 miles of water") reads like statute miles. If you want to be strict, add "nautical" once or keep everything at "about". The rounded figures are not wrong.
2. **Wikipedia's construction details are unreliable here.** WP-TTC gives "four layers of gutta-percha" and "ten steel wires". Both conflict with the engineer-in-chief's own specification (LSB p. 49: three layers; 18 strands of 7 iron wires). The script is right; do not "correct" it towards Wikipedia.
3. **The Whitehouse vs Thomson story.** The public dispute was carried on through BA papers (1855 and 1856) and letters in *The Athenaeum* (autumn 1856). Thomson was not at the 1856 Cheltenham meeting, so the scene should not show them face to face. The rush to lay in 1857 explains why a small-core cable was contracted before Thomson and Bright could change it. It does not explain why Whitehouse was "given the electrical side", which is the fix for frame 49.
4. **1857 departure point.** The 1857 cable was landed on the mainland at Ballycarberry Strand in Valentia Harbour. The 1858 cable came ashore on Valentia Island (Knightstown). If later chunks say "Agamemnon brought her end ashore at Valentia" for 1858, that is fine.
5. **Discrepancy inside the sources.** LSB prints "3.45 p.m." for the 11 Aug 1857 break. Cyrus Field's contemporary letter says 3:45 a.m. The a.m. time fits the "night" scene and Bright's report about tired night staff.
