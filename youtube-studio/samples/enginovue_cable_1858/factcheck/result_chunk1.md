## Notes (systemic / for other chunks)

1. **The failure date and "six weeks" recur.** Later narration says "Six weeks after landing, the line was dead." The sources say intelligible signals ended on 1 Sep 1858, under four weeks after the 5 Aug landing (JC1861, HMF, Hunt). One garbled final message is recorded on 20 Oct (JC1861 Q2582–2583; Bright1908 gives 20 Oct and 732 messages). I suggest "Within a month of landing, the line was all but dead." Use "September 1858" or "early September", never "18 September".
2. **Recurring "one word every ten minutes".** Later narration says "The 1858 cable had managed roughly one word every ten minutes." As a description of the cable's speed, this is DISPUTED. JC1861 (Varley) gives about 1 word per minute by relay and 2 words per minute on Thomson's galvanometer. WP-TAC gives about 0.1 words per minute. The 10-minute figure is only the Queen's-message average, stoppages included. Suggested later wording: "The Queen's message in 1858 had averaged about one word every ten minutes."
3. **"More than 2,000 volts" (later chunk).** Hunt has a footnote: "Many secondary sources (e.g., Thompson, *Kelvin*, 1: 385) say Whitehouse's induction coils produced tensions of 2000 volts, but cite no basis for this estimate." The chunk owner should mark it DISPUTED or soften it (e.g. "thousands of volts", or "very high voltages").
4. **Whitehouse's dismissal (later chunk).** Hunt: the board voted to dismiss him on 17 Aug 1858, while the cable was still working. This fits "Whitehouse was blamed and lost his post", but the order of events should not imply he was dismissed after the cable died.
5. **Not checked by me, flagged for later chunks.** WP-TAC says the core had "four layers of gutta-percha"; the script says three coats. WP-TAC says "Three hundred miles ... of cable were lost" in 1857; the script says the break came "about 380 miles out". Both need checking against Bright1908 and JC1861 by whoever has those frames.
6. **The date of the Queen's message is safe.** It started on 16 Aug and was completed in the early morning of 17 Aug. The directors' message, not the Queen's, was technically the first sent on 16 Aug (WP-TAC; JC1861 log 11.12 am). The script never calls the Queen's message "the first", so this is fine.
7. **Blocked sources.** atlantic-cable.com blocks automated access (403), so nothing here relies on it. JC1861 was used instead; it is the official 1861 report, with the signal log, that atlantic-cable.com itself transcribes.
