## Notes

1. **Timeline compression around frames #87–#105 creates real errors.** The actual order was as follows. On 5 Aug both ends landed. Whitehouse's coils and relays were in use in the first days, and the relays failed. On 10 Aug Valentia switched to Thomson's mirror galvanometer. Around 11 Aug Whitehouse mostly set his coils aside at Valentia, while de Sauty kept using coils at Trinity Bay. On 16 Aug the Queen's message was slowed by Whitehouse's unauthorised underrunning of the cable. On 17 Aug Whitehouse was dismissed for insubordination. From 21 Aug Thomson was in charge at Valentia. On 1 Sep the last recorded message passed. In early September the cable was effectively dead, and it was formally given up around 20 Oct 1858. The script tells this as galvanometer success, then Whitehouse "kept trying" with coils, then the cable dies, then Whitehouse is fired. The proposed VO changes for #99, #104 and #105 fix the order without changing frame timing.
2. **Same "six weeks" / "September 18" problem in chunk 1 (outside my frames).** The opening narration says "By September 18, 1858, it is dead" and "why did it die in six weeks?". No source I found supports 18 September. Sources give 1 Sep as the last recorded message (FIELD), "early September" (HUNT), "less than a month" (SMG), or 20 Oct 1858 as the formal stop (IET). The opening should be aligned, e.g. "By early September 1858, it is all but dead" / "why did it die in a month?".
3. **The voltage figure.** "2,000 volts" is widely repeated, but Hunt (2021) traces it to Thompson's *Kelvin* (1910) and says it has no basis. Varley's 1860 testimony implies about 10,000–15,000 V. "Thousands of volts" is defensible under every source.
4. **Causation.** The script should not imply the coils alone killed the cable. Hunt, Field, Wikipedia and the Science Museum Group all point to pre-existing manufacturing, storage and handling damage that the coils made worse. Hunt also notes the debate over whether Whitehouse was a scapegoat.
5. **Quotations.** No VO line in frames #77–#114 contains quotation marks, including #109–#113. Nothing here is presented as a direct quote, so there is no misquotation. If a quote is wanted for #106, "Was the Atlantic cable a humbug?" (a Boston paper, per FIELD) is genuine.
6. **Related item outside my frames.** Hunt p.92 says the Queen's message was reported as "ninety-nine words" taking "more than sixteen hours". That time was inflated by Whitehouse's underrunning interruption, so the actual sending time was "far less". The opening's "98 words" and "about sixteen hours… one word every ten minutes" (also repeated near the end of the narration) should be checked by the chunk-1 reviewer.
7. **Storm timing (frame #76, just before my chunk).** Wikipedia says "ten days out". Hunt says "three days out of Plymouth". Field says the first three days were fair and the gales lasted "more than a week", peaking on 20 June. The script's "on the way out" is safe.
