# Fact-check report — whatif_super_speed + Shorts (speed_kills, sonic_wall, corners)

Method: own calculations (calc_notes in script.md) + two independent fact-check passes (sections 0–3 with
Shorts 1 and 3; sections 4–7 with Short 2), 2026-11-22. Each claim was checked against the sources listed below.
Items marked "re-check at upload" can change.

## Corrections made after the fact-check
- **Ordinary grip cannot push a runner past ~170 km/h.** Friction is at most ~μmg ≈ 590–740 N, and air drag
  matches that at ~166–185 km/h. So the claims "45 s / 7.5 km to reach Mach 1" and "7.5 km to stop" were wrong
  and have been removed. Level 3 now states that super grip is required. Braking from Mach 1 by air drag alone is
  ~44 g. Short 3 was reworked into "Super speed needs super grip".
- "Stiff breeze" → "strong breeze" (Beaufort Force 6, 11–14 m/s).
- Reaction time: "a quarter of a second; for a surprise closer to a full second → 9 to 36 m".
- Turn radius: 160 → 170 m. "Two family cars" → "two mid-size cars".
- US supersonic ban: "for half a century" (14 CFR 91.817 since 1973). The FAA published an NPRM to repeal it on
  2 July 2026. **Re-check at upload.**
- Level 4: "as hot as a wood-fired pizza oven" (not "hotter than most"). SR-71 "almost entirely titanium" (NASA
  wording). Clothes: cotton ignites at ~255 °C, and the wind would already have torn them off at level 3.
- Level 6: the run moves to space, because 0.99c inside the air would be catastrophic. "Moon a bit more than a
  second away" replaces London–New York. Interstellar gas is "roughly one atom per cm³, often less". Edelstein &
  Edelstein 2012 give a practical limit of ~0.5c.
- Short 2: Stapp 46 g "recovered with no lasting injury". "Worst survived crash ≈ 200 g" (Bräck 214 g). The
  claim "the hardest stop survived on purpose" was removed (Beeding took 83 g deliberately in 1958).
- Short 1: the stumble line is reworded ("no helmet and no leathers") and adds the premise "your feet never slip".

## Verified claims (OK)
- Bolt ~12.4 m/s (44.7 km/h), Berlin 2009 (Graubner & Nixdorf 2011). Weyand et al. 2000: speed comes from
  ground force. Peak force several (up to ~5) × body weight (Clark & Weyand 2014).
- Saffir–Simpson: Cat 1 is 119–153 km/h; Cat 5 is ≥ 252 km/h.
- Speed of sound 343 m/s at 20 °C = 1,235 km/h (1,225 km/h at the 15 °C standard day).
- Running CdA 0.33–0.45 m² (Pugh 1971 and others). This gives > 3 t at Mach 1 as a lower bound, because Cd
  rises in the transonic range. Drag ratio (343/12.42)² ≈ 763.
- 11.1 MW → ~160,000 kcal/min ≈ 80 days at 2,000 kcal (FDA reference). This is a best case, since muscles are ~25 %
  efficient.
- Stagnation temperature at ~1,000 m/s: ≈ 513 °C.
- Orbit at the surface: 7.9 km/s ≈ 28,400–28,470 km/h. Shuttle TPS: 1,260 °C tiles, ~1,480 °C RCC.
- Massive bodies cannot reach c. γ(0.99c) = 7.09. Earth's orbital speed is 29.78 km/s.
- 12,000 g for 343 m/s → 0 over 0.5 m. Organs moving relative to the skeleton is the classic deceleration-injury
  mechanism (aortic isthmus).
