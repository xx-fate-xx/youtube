## Notes

- **Scene 121 is the only clear factual error.** The famous 2 August 1865 break happened at the bow during picking-up, not at the stern during paying-out. Both eyewitness and near-contemporary accounts (Russell, Field) agree on this. The Dudley lithograph "In the bows… August 2nd the cable broken and lost" (Royal Museums Greenwich / Met Museum) shows the bow setting.
- **The 1858 speed figure needs to match chunk 1.** "One word every ten minutes" depends on the 98-words-in-16-hours framing. Lindley (cited by Wikipedia) says 67 minutes for the inbound message and 16 hours for the confirmation copy. Russell gives a maximum working speed of up to 2.5 words a minute. Whatever chunk 1 decides about the Queen's message, frame 134 should use the same basis. The proposed "a single letter could take two minutes" is robust to either version.
- **Scene 127 (minor, optional):** in 1866 the cable was read with Thomson's mirror galvanometer, so a moving spot of light would match the earlier galvanometer segment better than a swinging needle. I did not verify which receiving instrument was used on 27 July 1866 at Heart's Content, so this is a suggestion, not a correction.
- **Brunel (116):** he died in 1859, before any cable work. The VO is correct. Make sure later frames never show Brunel on deck in 1865–66.
- **The 1865–66 grappling details:** in 1865 the cable was hooked three times. A fourth attempt on 10 August failed because a grapnel fluke fouled the chain, so the cable was never caught. In 1866 the work took about 30 grappling runs over roughly three weeks, with the Albany and Medway involved. The VO compresses all of this but doesn't misstate it.
- **Date nit (not in the VO):** Field writes "Saturday, the seventh, of September" 1866. 7 September 1866 was a Friday, and the shore end may have been landed on the 8th. The VO says only "September 1866", so no change is needed.
- **Modern statistics:** the script cites no cable count or length. If one is added later, TeleGeography's FAQ (2026) says "more than 600 active and planned submarine cables" and "over 1.5 million kilometers of submarine cables in service".
