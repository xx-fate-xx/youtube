# Short 3 — "Super Speed Needs Super Grip"  (9:16, ~45 s)

Parent: whatif_super_speed. (Was "corners"; reworked after the fact-check: ordinary grip cannot even push a
runner past ~170 km/h against air drag.)

VO: Super speed has a problem nobody talks about: grip.

VO: To run, your feet push back against the ground, and friction decides how hard. Good rubber on dry concrete
gives you a bit less than your own body weight.

VO: Around a hundred and seventy kilometres an hour, the air pushes you back just as hard. Past that, normal shoes
can't make you any faster. You'd just spin your feet.

VO: Corners are worse. With normal grip, the tightest turn at motorway speed is a circle about three hundred and
thirty metres across.

VO: And to take an ordinary street corner at the speed of sound, your shoes would have to grip with about twelve
hundred times your body weight.

VO: Super speed needs super grip. Full video on what super speed would really be like, linked below.

VISUAL (vertical): hero on a city pavement, feet slipping (dust puffs) as the HUD hits ~170 km/h; chalk balance
of two arrows (feet push vs. AIR) becoming equal; top-down chalk map: amber trail overshooting a corner; a
330 m circle; final card "≈ 1,200 × body weight".

Calc: max push μmg, μ ≈ 0.8 (0.6–0.85): 589 N at 75 kg; drag ½ρ·CdA·v² = 0.276·v² → equal at 46 m/s
(166 km/h; ~185 km/h at 94 kg). Turn: r = v²/(μg) = 166 m at 130 km/h (diameter 332 m). Street corner r ≈ 10 m
at 343 m/s: a = v²/r = 11,765 m/s² ≈ 1,200 g.
