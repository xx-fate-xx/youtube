Facts (high confidence unless noted):
- 1881 convention: time distribution only. 30 July 1886: treaty for "distribution de la force motrice à domicile" = legal start of power supply (B p.21).
- CPAC ("Compagnie Parisienne de l'Air Comprimé – procédés Victor Popp") from 21 Feb 1887; SUDAC name from 1949 (B pp.21–22). 16 Dec 1887: 40-year concession for mains in sewers/under streets (B p.22).
- Rue Saint-Fargeau plant (20th arr.): from 1881; by 1889 "considerably over 2000 horse-power" at peak; air at 5 atm (6 atm absolute); 300 mm cast-iron main partly hung in sewers (Kennedy, A pp.13–14). Closed 1934 (B).
- Quai de la Gare plant (13th arr.): built 1890–91, inaugurated Dec 1892; four Schneider triple-expansion engines of 1,800 hp (B pp.39–45). Planned ~10,000 hp (medium).
- Pressure: 6 atm absolute at plant, 3.5–5.5 atm absolute at users (Kennedy).
- Mains length (company figures, B): 1888 48 km; 1900 163.6 km; 1920 435 km; 1930 525 km; 1949 678 km; 1964 ~900 km; 1970 941 km.
- Customers: 1889 list of 225 installations, motors ½–50 hp (A p.50); 1900 1,368; 1910 4,385; 1930 7,241; 1949 9,365; ~10,000 almost reached early 1960s (B).
- Uses 1889 (Kennedy): lathes, saws, drills; carpenters, smiths, umbrella makers, bookbinders; sewing machines; confectioners, coffee roasters, billiard-ball makers; printing presses of Le Figaro and Le Petit Journal; air motors driving dynamos for electric lighting; a restaurant where the cold exhaust air cooled the beer cellar and froze carafes and ice; beer lifted from barrels by air (A pp.46, 50–51).
- Lifts: hydro-pneumatic lifts in western Paris from ~1900; gone by 1991 (B).
- Electricity: CPAC got an electricity sector in May 1889 (Richard-Lenoir station); by 1900 electricity was ~97% of CPAC's revenue; lost from 1914 (B pp.46–49, 67).
- Efficiency (Kennedy 1889): 10 hp at a motor 4 miles away for 20 ihp at the station with reheating (coke stove), 35 ihp without → ~50% with reheat; Unwin: 26% cold.
- Price 1889: 1.5 centimes per m³ (motors) (A p.52).
- Decline: peak 475.83 million m³ in 1973; 295.8 million by 1991 (B).
- End: concession ended 30 Jun/1 Jul 1994 (B pp.136–137); Quai de la Gare hall and chimney listed MH 29 Jun 1994; since 2007 part of the architecture school ENSA Paris-Val de Seine; SUDAC Air Service still exists (on-site compressed air; Veolia since 2021).
- Pneumatic post was a SEPARATE PTT system (1866–1984); CPAC never ran it.
- Birmingham had a short-lived compressed-air power company (1889–1892, medium-low); other cities only proposed schemes.
