Facts (high confidence unless noted):
- Victor Popp, born Vienna 5 Mar 1846, Austrian, later naturalised French; resigned Sept 1892 [SUDAC p.13–16]. Death year unverified.
- Inventor credited: Carl Albert Mayrhofer (1835–1891), Viennese telegraph engineer; Popp and clockmaker Ernst Resch designed/ran the clock system [PAYER, Wien Museum]; Mayrhofer attacked Popp in an 1880 brochure (credit disputed) [SUDAC p.13].
- Vienna: trial approved 25 Aug 1876; public service from noon 24 Feb 1877 (Schottenring, Herrengasse, Wipplingerstraße); ~3.5 years; judged too inaccurate, not renewed [PAYER, MAYR].
- Paris: silver medal Exposition 1878; trial authorised 12 Nov 1878 (pipes in sewers); public service inaugurated 15 March 1880 [SUDAC p.14; DIKEC].
- Concession: Conseil municipal 19 Jul 1881, Prefect 12 Aug 1881, 50 years from 1 Jul 1881, time distribution only, NOT exclusive [SUDAC pp.11,18–20]. 1887 CPAC: 40-year authorisation from 1 Mar 1887 [SUDAC p.69].
- Central station: 7 rue Sainte-Anne (1st arr.); compressors in basement, master clock upstairs; 10 main lines 27 mm, branches 6–20 mm, 0.75 bar [SUDAC pp.15,18].
- Counts: end 1880 — 14 candelabra clocks, 33 municipal dials, 1,475 private clocks [SUDAC p.18]; end 1881 ~4,000 clocks in 750 houses, ~32 km pipe (Berly via PF, medium); peak 1884 — 7,800 private dials, ~3,500 subscribers; 1926 — 5,018 dials, 919 subscribers; ~100 public dials (1894: 102) [SUDAC p.69].
- Pipes: 10 km trial; ~32 km 1881; ≤65 km from 1894; 62 km in 1926 [SUDAC p.69].
- Price 1879: 5 centimes/day first clock (18 F/yr), 4 c second, 3 c third+ [SUDAC p.17].
- Economics: 1880 revenue 16.90 F per dial vs cost 60.70 F; broke even only ~1904–09 [SUDAC].
- End: authorisation expired 1 Mar 1927; not renewed, "not profitable"; pipes removed by the city; customers sold to Maison Magneta (electric clocks); radio, talking clock, electric networks as context [SUDAC pp.68–69].
- Accuracy: 1881 public clocks agreed within 4 s (DIKEC citing Mém. Soc. ingénieurs civils 1881); convention required ±30 s of Observatory time [SUDAC p.20].
- 1910 flood: clocks stopped at 10:53 (PF, medium — see research 4).

Competitor claims: "created 1880 by Popp" partly wrong (Vienna 1877, Mayrhofer); "thousands in 5 years" confirmed (subscriber dials); "50-year contract" confirmed with caveats (company, non-exclusive, ended 1927 under 1887 authorisation); "30 cents a day" unverifiable → say "five centimes a day"; "ended 1927, electronic clocks" partly (unprofitable, not renewed; electric clocks/radio context).
