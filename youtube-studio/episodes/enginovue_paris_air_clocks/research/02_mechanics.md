Facts (high confidence unless noted):
- Station: steam engine driving piston compressors; storage reservoir ~8 m³ at 5 atmospheres [H] (Berly: four Sautter-Lemonnier compressors, 15–45 psi receivers — numbers disagree; use [H]).
- A regulator feeds a distributing reservoir at 7/10 of an atmosphere (~0.7 bar above atmospheric) [H, N, B].
- Every minute the mains are connected for 20 seconds, then vented to atmosphere for 40 seconds [H, N, B] ("at the sixtieth second of every minute").
- Master clock = two separate movements: left runs the pendulum clock, right works the slide valve (released by trips) [H]; eccentric rod [B]. No fan fly in sources.
- Self-winding: air pistons lift the weights of both movements every minute by exactly the amount they fell [H, B].
- Correction: compared with the Paris Observatory, a few seconds of gain/loss corrected by hand every day [H] (1880).
- Redundancy: duplicate clock and valve, switchable in seconds; manual tap for emergency pulses; alarms; pressure gauge per main [H, B].
- Mains: wrought-iron, 27 mm inside, laid in the sewers [H, B]; ten separate mains radiating from the centre [B, 1881]; independent so a fault does not spread [H].
- House pipes: lead 15 mm in buildings → 6 mm in apartments → 3 mm lead or silk-covered rubber to the clock [H, N, B].
- 1881: ~20 miles (~32 km) of mains + 177 miles of branches; 750 houses; >4,000 clocks; arrondissements 1 and 2 [B].
- Receiver: rubber bellows (concertina of soft rubber discs) [H, B]; bellows lifts a lever pivoted on the wheel axis; counterweighted pawl pushes a 60-tooth wheel one tooth; balanced detent stops it slipping back; stops limit travel so one pulse = exactly one tooth = one minute; lever falls back when the main is vented [B figs 10–12, H]. Hour hand via a small wheel train (12:1 implicit). Striking clocks: second bellows winds the strike.
- Missed pulse: no automatic correction (clock stays behind until reset) — inference; errors from vandalism, burst bellows [H].
- Dials from under 12 in to very large [B] (medium).
- Physics: pressure front ≈ speed of sound ≈ 340 m/s → ~3 s per km, ~15 s for 5 km; filling narrow pipes slows the rise → the 20 s window. 1879 Clocks Commission estimate ~20 s end to end [D]; 1881: public clocks within ~4 s [D]. Popp's "4–5 s at 5 km" is physically impossible — do not use.

Competitor claims: two movements CONFIRMED; fan fly/20 s run UNVERIFIABLE; 20 s open / 40 s CONFIRMED (40 s vented); self-winding CONFIRMED; ten mains CONFIRMED (1881); "hundreds of km through sewers and metro tunnels" MISLEADING (32 km mains 1881; metro opened 1900; metro/RER routing belongs to the later power network); "up to a minute" WRONG (~15–20 s); 60 teeth CONFIRMED (via lever and pawl); daily correction from Observatory CONFIRMED.
