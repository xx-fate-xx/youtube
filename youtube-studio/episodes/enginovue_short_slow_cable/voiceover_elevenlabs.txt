August 1858. With stops and repeats, Queen Victoria's message to America takes about sixteen hours. It's only about a hundred words. The cable is a copper core in insulation, lying in salt water. It soaks up charge, like a tank that must fill first. So a crisp tap from Ireland reaches Newfoundland as a blurred swell. In 1855, William Thomson showed the delay grows with the square of the length. Double the cable, four times the delay. His answer to faint signals: a tiny magnet and mirror on a silk thread. The faintest current swung a spot of light: one way a dot, the other a dash. But its insulation was already flawed, and huge high-voltage induction coils likely made it worse. Within a month, nothing readable got through. How they fixed it: full story on the channel.
