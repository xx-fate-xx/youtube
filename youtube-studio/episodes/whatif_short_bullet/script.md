VO: Can you catch a bullet? Yes. But not the way you think.

VO: Someone fires a pistol across the street.

VO: The bullet gets there eight times faster than your brain can even react.

VO: Your legs are fast enough. Your brain isn't.

VO: So let's speed up your brain too. Now the world crawls, and you can see it coming.

VO: Shove the person out of the way? A push that fast is a crash all by itself.

VO: Take it in the chest? You're unbreakable, so it bounces or breaks apart, and the pieces could hit someone else.

VO: Here's the move. Run right next to the bullet, match its speed, and gently close your hand around it.

VO: Everyone hears the gunshot, and then a second crack. That's you, going faster than sound.

VO: Four more rescues in the full video. And the next time someone asks...

Facts (parent factcheck_report.md):
- 9 mm 115 gr ≈ 1,180 ft/s, a little faster than sound (row 19); 30 ft in ≈ 1/40 s (calc_notes).
- Simple reaction ≈ 1/5 s (row 20) → 0.2 / 0.025 = 8 ("eight times faster than your brain can react").
- Shove 2 ft in 1/40 s ≈ 190 g (calc_notes) — about four times Stapp's 46 g and close to the highest crash
  survived (~214 g, Guinness, sonic_wall Short sources) → "a crash all by itself".
- Ricochet/fragments off hard surfaces are a real danger (row 21).
- Matching the bullet's speed means moving faster than sound → a sonic boom (NASA Dryden FS-016, parent sources).
